> **1. INTRODUCTION AND SCOPE**

**1.1 About this Policy**

Clauze (\"Clauze\", \"we\", \"us\", or \"our\") provides an
artificial-intelligence-powered legal workspace and contract lifecycle
management platform. This Privacy Policy explains how we collect, use,
disclose, retain, and protect personal data, and describes the rights
available to individuals whose personal data we process.

We are committed to processing personal data lawfully, fairly, and
transparently. This Policy is written to be read and understood --- not
to obscure our practices behind legal formality.

**1.2 Who this Policy applies to**

This Policy applies to personal data we process in connection with:

-   Visitors to our website, marketing pages, and public resources;

-   Individuals who register for, evaluate, or use the Clauze platform;

-   Authorised users acting on behalf of a subscribing organisation
    (\"Customer\");

-   Individuals who contact us for support, sales enquiries, or other
    purposes;

-   Prospective employees and applicants;

-   Third-party counterparties invited to a shared workspace, data room,
    or document exchange by a Customer.

Customer Content may incidentally contain Sensitive Personal Data ---
for example, health information in an employment file or an insurance

responsible for ensuring that any additional legal conditions applicable
to sensitive categories are satisfied.

We ask that you do not submit Sensitive Personal Data through support
channels, sales enquiries, or free-text fields unless it is strictly
necessary and you have a lawful basis for doing so.

> **5. ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE AND AUTOMATED PROCESSING**

Clauze uses artificial intelligence to analyse documents, extract
structured information, identify risks and obligations, detect conflicts
between agreements, and assist with drafting and review. This section
explains how that works and what safeguards apply.

**5.1 We do not train shared models on Customer Content**

> **Customer Content is never used to train, fine-tune, or otherwise
> improve any artificial intelligence model that is shared with, or
> accessible to, any other customer. This commitment is contractual and
> appears in every Clauze Data Processing Agreement.**

Where a Customer expressly elects, in writing, to enable a
tenant-specific model or knowledge base derived from its own content,
that model remains logically isolated to that Customer\'s tenant and is
not accessible to any other party.

**5.2 How AI processing works**

-   Documents uploaded to the platform are processed to extract
    structured information --- parties, dates, values, obligations,
    governing law, and similar attributes.

-   Extracted information is stored as structured records within the
    Customer\'s isolated tenant.

-   When an Authorised User asks a question, the system retrieves
    relevant content from documents that user is permitted to access and
    generates a response grounded in that content.

-   Every AI-generated response cites the source clause or document it
    relies upon. Where the available content does not answer a question,
    the system states this rather than generating an unsupported answer.

**5.3 Access controls apply to AI features**

AI features respect the same permission model as the rest of the
platform. The assistant can only access documents that the requesting
user is already authorised to access. AI interactions are recorded in
the audit trail with the user identity, the documents accessed, and the
timestamp.

**5.4 Automated decision-making**

Clauze does not make decisions producing legal or similarly significant
effects concerning individuals on a solely automated basis. AI outputs
are informational and are intended to support --- not replace ---
professional judgement.

> **Clauze is a software platform, not a law firm. AI-generated output
> does not constitute legal advice and should not be relied upon as
> such. Customers should obtain advice from qualified counsel before
> acting on any output.**

**5.5 AI sub-processors**

We engage third-party providers of large language models and related
infrastructure to deliver AI functionality. These providers are engaged
as sub-processors under written terms that prohibit the use of Customer
Content for training their own models and that require processing
consistent with the residency commitments described in Section 8.

**7.1 Personal data breach**

We maintain a documented incident response procedure. Where a personal
data breach occurs:

-   We will notify the competent supervisory authority within the period
    required by applicable law --- including within seventy-two (72)
    hours where the GDPR applies, and within the period prescribed by
    the PDPL Implementing Regulations where KSA law applies.

-   Where Clauze acts as a Processor, we will notify the affected
    Customer without undue delay after becoming aware, and provide the
    information reasonably required for that Customer to meet its own
    notification obligations.

-   Where the breach is likely to result in a high risk to the rights
    and freedoms of individuals, affected individuals will be notified
    in accordance with applicable law.

No security measure is absolute. While we work continuously to protect
personal data, we cannot guarantee that unauthorised access will never
occur.

The provisions below supplement this Policy and apply where the relevant

**13.1 Kingdom of Saudi Arabia --- PDPL**

Where processing is subject to the Personal Data Protection Law (Royal
Decree M/19, 1443H) and its Implementing Regulations:

-   The supervisory authority is the Saudi Data and Artificial
    Intelligence Authority (SDAIA).

-   We maintain a Record of Processing Activities as required by the
    PDPL and make it available to SDAIA on request.

-   Personal data of individuals in the Kingdom is stored and processed
    within the Kingdom where the Customer\'s subscription specifies
    in-Kingdom residency.

-   Transfers outside the Kingdom are made only where permitted by the
    PDPL and its Implementing Regulations, and only where the transfer
    does not prejudice national security or vital interests of the
    Kingdom.

-   We do not process personal data for direct marketing without prior
    consent, and every marketing communication includes a means of
    opting out.

-   Where required, we appoint a Data Protection Officer and register
    with SDAIA in accordance with the applicable thresholds.

-   Data subjects may lodge a complaint with SDAIA. Contact details are
    published at sdaia.gov.sa.

**13.2 United Arab Emirates**

Where processing is subject to UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 on
the Protection of Personal Data, or to the data protection law of a
financial free zone:

-   For processing within the Dubai International Financial Centre, the
    DIFC Data Protection Law No. 5 of 2020 applies and the DIFC
    Commissioner of Data Protection is the supervisory authority.

-   For processing within the Abu Dhabi Global Market, the ADGM Data
    Protection Regulations 2021 apply and the ADGM Office of Data
    Protection is the supervisory authority.

-   For onshore processing, the UAE Data Office is the competent
    authority.

-   Cross-border transfers are made only in accordance with the
    conditions set out in the applicable law.

**13.3 Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan**

Where processing is subject to the Jordanian Personal Data Protection
Law No. 24 of 2023:

-   We comply with the registration and notification requirements
    applicable to controllers and processors.

-   We appoint a Data Protection Officer where the thresholds under the
    law are met.

-   Cross-border transfers are made only where the conditions under the
    law are satisfied.

-   The Personal Data Protection Council is the competent authority for
    complaints.

**13.4 European Economic Area and United Kingdom**

Where the GDPR or UK GDPR applies:

-   We will appoint a representative in the EEA or United Kingdom where
    Article 27 requires it, and publish the representative\'s contact
    details.

-   Transfers out of the EEA or United Kingdom rely on an adequacy
    decision, standard contractual clauses, the UK International Data
    Transfer Agreement or Addendum, or another approved mechanism,
    supplemented by a transfer impact assessment where appropriate.

-   Data subjects have the right to lodge a complaint with their
    national supervisory authority, or with the Information
    Commissioner\'s Office in the United Kingdom.
